# Changelog

All notable changes to Controls are documented here.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Each phase ships a minor release (`0.N.0`); fixes ship as patches. The save-format `schemaVersion` and the `catalogVersion` are versioned independently and recorded in [`docs/history.md`](docs/history.md).

- **How well a wire is landed now matters** (P3 F3.3 part 1; [ADR 0018](docs/decisions/0018-conductor-and-termination-physics.md#implementation-notes-f33-part-1), [simulation](docs/architecture/simulation.md#contact-resistance-f33-part-1)). - **The AC circuit solver** (F2.2 part 2, [simulation architecture](docs/architecture/simulation.md#the-phasor-solver-f22-part-2)). `@controls/sim-core` can now solve mains circuits: `AcSolver` works out every voltage and current of an AC island as a **phasor** (an RMS magnitude and a phase) at the region's 60 Hz, by modified nodal analysis in complex arithmetic, with a complex dense LU for small islands and a complex sparse LU (the same AMD ordering and factorisation cache as DC) from 64 unknowns. It handles resistors, inductors (−j/ωL), capacitors (jωC) and phasor voltage sources behind an internal resistance, at 0°, 180° and ±120°, the angles of a split-phase or three-phase supply, taken from exact constants so results are bit-identical in Node and Chromium. Inductance and capacitance are value modes of an ordinary resistive branch, so no netlist or `topologyHash` changes. DC and AC islands share one solution record (DC first, then AC); an island holding both kinds of source stays a diagnostic and is not solved, since P2 has no converters. Two ideal sources in parallel are flagged `singular`, exactly as in DC. Checked against hand calculations (a series RL circuit at 60 Hz, an unbalanced 120/240 V split-phase load whose neutral carries the phasor sum of the line currents, and a balanced 208Y/120 V wye load whose neutral carries about nothing), by property tests (Kirchhoff's current law with complex magnitudes, the real-power balance and the reactive-power bookkeeping, linearity and superposition, dense against sparse), and **against ngspice on all 500 AC oracle circuits**, the worst value using 1.8e-5 of the allowed error for volts and 5.0e-4 for amps, on both LU paths. - **The sim clock and engine loop** (F2.3a, [simulation architecture](docs/architecture/simulation.md#the-engine-loop-f23a), [ADR 0011](docs/decisions/0011-sim-worker-contract.md)). The simulator now runs: `SimEngine` in `@controls/sim-core` follows every command at its tick and lets sim time pass at 1 tick = 1 ms. Each tick it solves the circuit every 10 ticks, then runs the device layers in a fixed order (discrete, then continuous, such as a filament heating or a battery discharging, then protection, such as a fuse melting); a command that changes the circuit (closing a switch, connecting a lead) is solved at once, and a fuse that blows is re-solved in the same tick. Devices plug in as banks of preallocated state, rebuilt from their saved state at every change, with per-device tolerances drawn from the world's seed; the real battery, lamp and fuse models arrive in F2.4 and F2.5, so the hooks are proven with synthetic test devices. In scripts and console files: `game.advance(ms)`, `game.advanceTo(tick)` and `sim.step` let time pass (time passing is never undone and never logged), `game.probe('1:pos', '1:neg')` reads an ideal voltmeter between two terminals, `game.islands()` says which circuits are solved, `game.snapshot()` gives the simulation's published bytes, and `game.on('sim')` reports events (a fuse blowing, a circuit becoming singular or failing to settle). Circuits are never shown as a believable reading when they aren't one: two terminals on separate circuits read `floating`, ideal sources fighting read `singular`, a solve that didn't settle reads `unsettled`, and circuits the simulator can't solve yet (AC until the phasor solver, anything holding the lamp until its filament model) read `unsolved`. Saves and `world.hash` now hold the simulator's state, so a save at any tick loads and runs on exactly like the original, and the same seed and log give byte-identical snapshots in two runs, in a replay, and in Node and the browser (pinned by `apps/sim-cli/scenarios/sim-step.console` in both). The sim time commands `sim.setSpeed`, `sim.resume` and `sim.step` are now registered (there is no `sim.pause`: speed 0 pauses); pacing and the time controls arrive with the sim worker (F2.3b). `sim bench` gates one tick's device layers at the spec's 0.3 ms (about 0.015 ms for 250 loops of synthetic test devices). `@controls/sim-protocol` holds the snapshot codec, sim events and `materialiseWorld`.
- **The engine solves AC circuits** (F2.3a with F2.2 part 2's phasor solver). - **The DC circuit solver** (F2.2 part 1, [simulation architecture](docs/architecture/simulation.md#the-dc-solver-f22-part-1)). `@controls/sim-core` can now work out the voltage at every terminal and the current in every lead, cell and lamp of a DC circuit: `DcSolver` solves each separate circuit (island) of the netlist by modified nodal analysis, with a dense LU factorisation using partial pivoting. It handles resistances, voltage sources with internal resistance (a battery's 23 mΩ) and current sources. A lamp left dangling off a battery, or one sitting on its own, reads 0 V across and 0 A through it, as it should. Two ideal sources wired straight across each other have no solution: that circuit is flagged `singular` and shows zeros instead of garbage, while other circuits on the bench still solve. Every result is reproducible bit for bit, in the netlist's canonical order, ready for the save's solution record. Values come from a hook that the battery and lamp models (F2.4, F2.5) will fill; until then a battery uses its nominal 12 V. Checked against hand calculations (a divider, a Wheatstone bridge, a battery into a lamp, two unequal batteries in parallel, floating circuits) and by property tests that Kirchhoff's current law and the power balance hold on random circuits. Not wired into the game yet (F2.3a).
- **Sparse LU, the factorisation cache and the nonlinear outer loop** (F2.2 part 3, [simulation architecture](docs/architecture/simulation.md#sparse-lu-and-the-factorisation-cache-f22-part-3)). Large circuits now solve fast enough for a plant: an island of 64 or more unknowns is solved by a sparse LU factorisation (Gilbert–Peierls, with threshold partial pivoting) in an approximate-minimum-degree (AMD) column order, both written from the published papers; smaller ones stay on the dense solver, bit for bit as before (the pinned 300-solve hash is unchanged). Each island's symbolic factorisation (its matrix pattern, the AMD order and a recorded stamp program) is kept in a **factorisation cache** keyed by `topologyHash`, a 16-topology least-recently-used cache: flipping a switch back to a position the cache has seen, or changing only values (a lamp's resistance, a supply's setpoint), skips rebuilding it, and the numeric factorisation replays the previous pivot sequence whenever the pivot rule would choose the same rows. Results are bit-identical whether the cache is cold, warm, shared or thrashed, and across solver instances, including after values change (sources switching between ideal and non-ideal); counters (`cache.stats`, `sparse.replays`) show the hits. The sparse path's bits are pinned by their own SHA-256 over 60 seeded large solves, beside the dense path's 300-solve pin. `DcSolver.solveNonlinear` adds the **outer loop** that F2.4's CV/CC supply and F2.5's lamp R(T) will use: a value hook per iteration, a warm start from the previous solution record, damping that halves on oscillation and recovers, a 50-iteration cap, and the island's `nonConverged` flag when it doesn't settle. Each current must settle to its own rounding (per branch, so a small current on a stiff busbar island isn't let off early). A `nonConverged` island's values are the last solve's and look real, so later features must show it as unsettled. Checked by property tests: dense and sparse agree (residuals on every circuit, values to 1e-9 on well-conditioned ones, up to 260 nodes), the DC oracle passes on both paths, floating clusters stay structurally pinned on the sparse path, the cache matches a reference LRU model, and the loop reaches closed-form fixed points and stays within 40 iterations (median 16) on random bench worlds with self-heating lamps. On the 2,000-node bench plant a re-solve after a switch toggle (compile, values and solve) takes about 1.5 ms locally with a warm cache and 4.6 ms for a topology never seen; `sim bench` reports both (median and p90), and the 2 ms gate stays reported-only: CI measured 2.12, 3.74 and 4.77 ms (Ubuntu, macOS, Windows), so the recompile is optimised next and the gate then switches on as a ratio to a calibration loop timed in the same process (plan F2.2 part 3b).
- **The ngspice oracle corpus and the 2,000-node bench plant** (F2.7, [ADR 0012](docs/decisions/0012-oracle-corpus.md), accepted for DC; [oracle guide](docs/guides/oracle.md)). The circuit solver will be checked against ngspice, a long-established circuit simulator, on 1,000 random circuits: 500 DC (resistors, batteries with internal resistance, current sources) and 500 AC at 60 Hz (resistors, inductors, capacitors and phasor sources at 0°, 180° and ±120°), each with 2 to 30 nodes and built so that every element shares a loop with a source (about 99 % of branches carry real current). A seeded generator in `@controls/sim-core/oracle` writes each circuit both as a SPICE deck and as a sim-core netlist, so the two can't describe different circuits. The decks and ngspice 47's results (17 significant digits, Sparse solver, `gmin` matched) are checked in under `libs/domain/sim-core/oracle/` (about 3.4 MB of text), so `pnpm verify` never needs ngspice: it checks that the decks regenerate byte for byte from the pinned seeds, that the results came from those exact decks, and that ngspice's answers satisfy every element's equation and Kirchhoff's current law in sim-core's netlist (worst residual 3e-9). `pnpm sim oracle regen` regenerates the corpus with an installed ngspice and fails clearly without one. ### Fixed

- **Continue resumes the game you were last in.** Loading a save over a game with unsaved changes autosaves that game first, and if you then quit without changing the loaded save, Continue used to load that newer autosave instead of the game you were playing. Continue now remembers the save you last loaded, saved or autosaved (`saves.lastPlayed` in settings, `settingsVersion` 2; older settings files migrate) and tries it first, falling back to the newest save that loads, with the usual warning if the remembered save is damaged or gone. A game opened from a file outside the save folder, or saved only with Save As, now counts as unsaved until autosave copies it into the save folder, so Continue can resume it too. Tested in unit specs for each case, and end to end in the web harness and the desktop app ([ADR 0009](docs/decisions/0009-save-io-boundary.md#continue-and-the-last-played-save)).
- **CI and release failure artifacts now contain the Playwright report and traces.** Playwright writes them under `dist/.playwright`, and `upload-artifact` skips everything inside a dot-directory by default, so failed runs uploaded nothing (`No files were found with the provided path: dist/.playwright`) and the release logs held only `builder-debug.yml`. Both uploads now set `include-hidden-files: true`, and the release artifact name carries the run id and attempt, like CI's, so re-running a failed job can't collide with the earlier attempt's artifact.

- A flaky `world-model` property test: its reference canonical-JSON builder put integer-like object keys (`"0"`) first, as JavaScript objects always do, and so disagreed with `world.hash`'s correct code-unit order on some generated worlds. The hash itself was right; the test now builds its reference string directly.
- **A setting changed just before quitting is no longer lost.** Settings are saved 300 ms after the last change, and that save was dropped if you quit (menu Quit, Cmd+Q, closing the window) inside those 300 ms. The desktop app now asks the page to finish its pending writes before a window closes or the app quits, and waits for it (up to 2 s, so a hung page can't stop you quitting). The browser harness writes pending settings when the tab is closed or reloaded. This also caused the intermittent macOS CI failure of the settings-persistence test, which now waits for both changes to be on disk and has regression tests for quitting and for closing the window straight after a change.

Navigation, pop-ups, `<webview>` and permission requests are denied, and `https://` links open in the system browser. Electron Fuses disable RunAsNode, `NODE_OPTIONS` and the inspect flags, and turn on cookie encryption and ASAR integrity. Only one instance of the app can run at a time.
- A typed, Zod-validated IPC skeleton (`@controls/ipc-contract`). One `app:info` channel, which accepts calls only from the app's own origin, is exposed to the renderer as `window.controls.getAppInfo()`. The preload imports a narrow `@controls/ipc-contract/preload` entry, so Zod stays out of it.
- A browser dev harness (`pnpm start:web`) that runs the same Angular 22 zoneless app with `@controls/platform-web` instead of the Electron bridge.
- Developer workspace: Nx 24 + pnpm 11, TypeScript 6.0 strict, ESLint flat config with module-boundary tags (`type:app`, `type:feature`, `type:ui`, `type:domain`, `type:rendering`, `type:platform`, `type:contract`, `type:e2e`), Prettier, and Node 24 pinned in `.nvmrc`.
- `pnpm verify`, which runs exactly what CI runs: Prettier check, lint, typecheck, unit tests, build and the version-sync check. `pnpm version:sync` copies the root version into the desktop app and `@controls/units`.
- End-to-end tests: `apps/game-e2e` (Playwright against the web harness, including a check that the viewport isn't blank) and `apps/desktop-e2e` (an Electron smoke suite that runs against the built app, or against a packaged binary via `CONTROLS_E2E_EXECUTABLE`).
- GitHub Actions: `pr.yml` (verify and both e2e suites on Linux), `main.yml` (the same on Windows, macOS and Linux) and `release.yml` (on a `v*.*.*` tag: check the tag matches the version, verify, package each OS, smoke-test the packaged app, and only then upload it to a **draft** GitHub Release with `latest*.yml`). All three share the `.github/actions/setup` composite action.
- Documentation skeleton: README, this changelog, the docs hub, architecture overview, caveats, ADRs 0001–0004, glossary, history, new-developer and releasing guides, `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` and the MIT licence.
- Claude Code setup: `CLAUDE.md`, the `docs-keeper`, `version-keeper`, `release-engineer`, `electron-platform-engineer` and `test-engineer` agents, and the `/verify`, `/docs-sync` and `/release` skills.
