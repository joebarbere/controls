## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-16

Phase 3, "Pull, strip, land": wires, terminations, enclosures and hand tools. Mount boxes and a DIN-rail panel, run NM-B cable along the studs, strip and land every conductor with the right tool, and flip the breaker to light a 120 V lamp. Conductors heat with the current, and a loose termination shows a measurable voltage drop, runs hot and degrades. Saves move to `schemaVersion` 3 and the catalogue to `catalogVersion` 2; every 0.3.0 save still loads.

- **NM-B cable runs: staples, bored studs, stripped jackets and conductor tails** (P3 F3.2 part 4, domain and renderer half; [commands](../../docs/architecture/commands.md#cables-in-framing-f32-part-4), [rendering](../../docs/architecture/rendering.md#cables-f32-part-4)). A cable run in a stud wall (a route with a staple or a bored hole, or any run between two boxes on the wall) must be stapled within 304.8 mm (12 in) of each box (a bored hole doesn't count there) and supported at most every 1371.6 mm (4-1/2 ft) along the run, as NFPA 70 Art. 334.30 (by number) asks; `cable.add` and `cable.reroute` refuse a longer gap as `staple-spacing`. A drill bit or hole saw wider than 35.56 mm (40 % of a 2×4 stud's depth, IRC R602.6 by number, every P3 wall taken as exterior or bearing with single studs) can't bore a stud (`beyond-tool-capacity`), and the snapper won't offer a bore with one. A centred 1 in hole leaves exactly 31.75 mm to the stud face; anything wider needs a nail plate (NFPA 70 Art. 300.4(A)(1) by number), which P3 doesn't model, so it is a warning for the UI (`boreNeedsNailPlate`) rather than a refusal. Stripping the jacket now decides where each conductor can reach: the jacket runs straight in from the entry and ends where the strip begins, and a cable conductor lands only on a terminal within the stripped length of that point (`tail-too-short` otherwise); at least 6.35 mm (1/4 in) of jacket stays inside a box (NFPA 70 Art. 314.17 by number; `cable.strip` refuses more as `strip-too-long`). A save holding a world that breaks any of these rules is refused at load, as the commands would refuse it. The viewport draws NM-B flat at its real width × height (up a stud, with its wide face against the stud), from where the jacket ends in one box to where it ends in the other, and each conductor's tail by itself in its own insulation colour (bare copper for the ground) out to its terminal, or as a stub as long as the strip; under the voltage, current and heat overlays the tails take their own conductor's colours while the jacket stays "no reading". A labelled cable conductor gets a sleeve on each tail near its end (the text stays data, not drawn).
- Stranded conductors are drawn (and packed in ducts) at their real width: a concentric bundle of n strands in k layers is (2k + 1)/√n times the solid diameter (3/√7 for 7 strands), so a stranded 14 AWG THHN is about 0.2 mm wider than solid; bunched counts (MTW's 16 and 26 strands) take the 7-strand factor. No routing golden moved (the parity world is solid THHN).
