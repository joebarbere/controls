## Context

P2's split-phase services split the full winding's impedance equally between the two lines, so a bolted line-to-neutral fault at the terminals drew exactly what a line-to-line one does. A real 120/240 V centre-tapped distribution transformer's half winding has less than half the full winding's impedance, so its line-to-neutral fault current is the higher one (about 1.5× in short-circuit guides). That was acceptable while the services were headless with no protective devices; P4's house has breakers whose magnetic pickup depends on it (the bolted-short acceptance), so the simulation caveats made adopting a T-equivalent a P4 obligation.

P4 also makes bonding physical. ADR 0019 decided that the compiler reads three world relations, `mountedOn` (a breaker on a bus stab, a jumper on a load centre, a yoke on a metal box), `pluggedInto` (an appliance's plug in an outlet) and, in a later design, `inEntry`, and that it adds an implicit remote-earth node reached by sourced earth resistances. It left the representation of the T-equivalent and the physics notes to this ADR.

## Decision (part 1)

### 1. The split-phase T-equivalent, as a coupled 2 × 2 source impedance

For a 120/240 V split-phase source of rating S, %Z and X/R, on its own base (V_LL = 2 V_LN, Z_base = V_LL² / S, never a table):

- the full winding Z_full = R + jX, |Z_full| = (%Z / 100) Z_base, R = |Z_full| / √(1 + (X/R)²);
- each line's T arm Z_h = Z_full / 2;
- the half winding's own impedance Z_LN = 1.5 R_pu + j 1.2 X_pu on the 120 V base, which is **0.375 R + j0.3 X of the full winding in ohms** (the base is (V_LN / V_LL)² = 1/4 of the 240 V one, exactly);
- the neutral arm Z_n = Z_LN − Z_h = −0.125 R − j0.2 X, **negative**.

Z_n is never a branch. An internal centre-tap node with a negative neutral element would break the AC solver's singularity argument and the stamps' R > 0, L > 0 validation (ADR 0019 §7). The neutral arm is instead **eliminated into the two windings' source rows**: each winding is a `vsource` from the transformer neutral to its line behind its own Z_h + Z_n = Z_LN, and its row also carries Z_n times the other winding's current:

```text
V_l1 − V_x0 + Z_LN · I_1 + Z_n · I_2 = E_1          (E_1 = 120∠0°)
V_l2 − V_x0 + Z_n · I_1 + Z_LN · I_2 = E_2          (E_2 = 120∠180°)
```

which is exactly the T with its centre node eliminated (the current into the neutral terminal is I_1 + I_2, and it flows through Z_n). Its terminal behaviour is ADR 0019's: a lone line-to-neutral fault sees Z_LN, a line-to-line fault (I_2 = −I_1) sees 2 (Z_LN − Z_n) = Z_full.

- **The solver.** `AcBranchValues.couple(branch, partner, R_m, L_m)` gives a `vsource` row a mutual impedance R_m + jωL_m on its partner's current (either sign). The phasor solver stamps it as one more matrix entry in the source row (value code `VALUE_ZM`), on the dense and the sparse path alike. Every coupled pair is checked at each solve (cold, a few comparisons): the partner is a `vsource` of the same island coupled back with the same values, and the pair's real block [[R_a, R_m], [R_m, R_b]] is **positive definite** (R_a, R_b > 0 and R_a R_b > R_m²). A pairing that fails is a device-model error and throws.
- **The singularity argument holds.** The source block Z is complex symmetric, Z = R + jX with R and X real symmetric, so Re(Iᴴ Z I) = Iᴴ R I > 0 for I ≠ 0 when R is positive definite. For the split-phase block R = [[R_h + R_n, R_n], [R_n, R_h + R_n]], whose eigenvalues are R_h and R_h + 2 R_n = 0.25 R (positive). A coupled pair has R > 0, so it is never part of a loop of ideal sources, and "singular ⇔ a loop of ideal sources" is unchanged (`mna/ac.ts`).
- **The factorisation cache.** A coupled source's row has one more entry, so it has its own pattern class (`CLASS_SOURCE_COUPLED`) in the cache signature: a cached symbolic factorisation is never reused across a coupling change.
- **Every split-phase source** takes it: the P3 stand-in `utility.splitPhase` (the workshop and old saves), the new `service.splitPhase`, and the generator stub (`generator.portable`). The factors are for typical single-phase centre-tapped distribution transformers (Kersting's approximation); **on the generator stub they are a labelled abstraction** (its alternator has no centre-tapped winding of that construction).
- **No `topologyHash` moves.** The netlist's elements, kinds and ends are unchanged: the coupling is a value. The constants are `SPLIT_PHASE_HALF_WINDING_R_FACTOR` (1.5) and `SPLIT_PHASE_HALF_WINDING_X_FACTOR` (1.2) in `sim-core` (no catalogue change: ADR 0019 D4).

**Golden** (the pre-audit's, recomputed with complex phasors in `ac-source.spec.ts`): the 25 kVA, 2 %, X/R 2 unit has Z_h = 10.304 + j20.608 mΩ, Z_n = −2.576 − j8.243 mΩ, Z_LN = 7.728 + j12.365 mΩ (|Z_LN| = 14.581 mΩ), so **I_LN = 120 / 0.014581 = 8229.96 A** and **I_LL = 240 / 0.04608 = 5208.33 A**, ratio **1.580**. The engine reproduces both within 1e-5 at a 17 nΩ busbar.

### 2. The service (`service.splitPhase`)

A new model kind: the pole transformer, the service drop and the meter base as one entity (D4). Terminals `l1`, `l2`, `n` (the meter base's load-side lugs) and `x0` (the transformer's neutral bushing at the pole, which an entry declares `access: 'none'`). No state.

- **The transformer** is §1's T-equivalent, its windings from `x0`.
- **The drop** is three conductors of `dropLengthMm`, each R = ρ₀ · L / A · (1 + α (T − T₀)) at the game's 20 °C ambient (`serviceDropOhm`; resistivity × length ÷ area, never a table). Each line conductor's resistance is added to its winding's own R (the same as a series resistor, with no node between); the neutral's is a resistor element `dropN` from `x0` to `n`. **Abstractions:** the drop's reactance is left out, with a stated bound: Omitting drop reactance (≈ 0.1 mΩ/m per conductor for triplex) overstates the bolted fault at the meter by ≤ 5 % for drops of 2 AWG Al and smaller up to 100 m, and by up to about 20 % at 4/0; P4 content uses 2 AWG or smaller, and a larger drop needs its reactance modelled or the bound restated. The test drop (30 m of 2 AWG and 4 AWG aluminium) is overstated by about 1.7 % line to neutral and 3.8 % line to line, and a branch circuit's current by under 0.1 %. The drop has no thermal node in P4.
- **The utility's neutral grounding** is an earth link from `x0` (§3) of `utilityGroundingOhm`.
- Its island takes the 120/240 V nominal band, as the stand-in's does.

The content entry (25 kVA, a sourced drop, meter-base lugs) is P4 PR 14's; this PR tests the kind on a test entry.

### 3. Remote earth and ground rods (D5)

- **The earth node** is an implicit one-terminal pseudo-fragment at **entity 0, terminal `earth`**, never in the world, present only when an earth link compiles. Plant terminal 0 is reserved for it, so when it is present it is **node 0** and every other node's index is one higher; with no link the netlist is exactly engine 7's. Readers never look entity 0 up (the netlist panel names it "remote earth"); the engine leaves it out of an island's entity list.
- **Earth links** are declared by a netlist model (`DeviceTopology.earth`: a contact id, the device's terminal, `resistanceOhm`) and compiled as relation contacts of relation `earth` (§4) from that terminal to earth. P4 has two: the service's `grounding` link from `x0` (`utilityGroundingOhm`) and a ground rod's `electrode` link from its acorn clamp.
- **`electrode.groundRod`** is a new model kind: one terminal `clamp`, parameter `earthResistanceOhm`, no state. The rod's resistance is **computed offline** by Dwight's single-rod formula with the radius (IEEE Std 142, cited by number), R = ρ / (2π L) · (ln(4L / a) − 1): an 8 ft × 5/8 in rod at ρ = 100 Ω·m is **39.90 Ω** (with the diameter, the must-avoid, it would be 35.38 Ω). A spec recomputes it with `Math.log`; sim code never evaluates a logarithm (D6). The entry (PR 15) carries the value and the calculation in its source.
- **The utility's grounding resistance is 5 Ω**, game-design inside a sourced 1–25 Ω range (pre-audit), a parameter of the service entry.
- **Earth is not an effective fault path, and the model shows it rather than assuming it.** A line faulted into a separate rod returns through remote earth to the transformer neutral by the utility's grounding in parallel with the service's own rod (through its GEC, the main bonding jumper and the neutral): I = V / (R_rod + R_utility ∥ R_service-rod), **2.704 A** at 39.9 Ω, 5 Ω and 39.9 Ω (the plan's ≈ 2.70 A, Art. 250.4(A)(5) by number), which trips nothing. A fault into the service's own rod returns through the GEC and the bond instead (about 1.54 kA (1539.0 A) on the test service: 120 / |Z_LN + R_L + R_bb + (R_metal ∥ 44.9 Ω)| with R_metal = 2 R_bond + R_N + 2 R_bb). Both are goldens in `service.spec.ts`.
- **Caveat (fix before P6, ADR 0019):** an earth or EGC path that joins a DC circuit to an AC island makes the island `mixed` (unsolved). P4 has no such circuit.

### 4. Relation contacts

ADR 0019's rules, as implemented:

- **Mount contacts** (`mountedOn`): for each contact of the device's fit for the host mount's type, by contact id. On a bus mount (`bus` non-null) only `host: 'stab'` contacts count, and the stab is **`busStabIndex(host, mount, position, fit)` plus the contact's `positionOffset`**, its leg **`busStabLeg(mount, stab)`** (world-model's functions, called, never recomputed). On any other mount the host contact must be one the mount offers (`EnclosureMount.contacts`), mapped to its terminal. A side that declares nothing adds nothing and reports nothing (every P3 entry, a DIN rail, a yoke on a nonmetallic box); contact-less fits exit early.
- **Plug contacts** (`pluggedInto`): one per pin of the appliance's plug, by pin id, from the plug pin's terminal to the outlet's slot terminal for that pin, each of the outlet's `contactOhm`. A two-blade 1-15P makes two.
- **Each contact is one resistor branch** of model `contact`, from the dependent device's terminal (end `a`) to the host's (end `b`), valued by its `resistanceOhm` (DC and AC defaults, the resistor-default fast path), **never an ideal join**, so bus, bond and plug currents are in the solution. No thermal node on a contact in P4 (an abstraction; ADR 0019 §9).
- **Absent contacts.** When both sides declare a contact but an end's terminal isn't netlisted (its entity has no netlist model, or lacks the terminal), or a bus contact has no stab, the contact is absent with the diagnostic `absent-contact` (`entity`, `relation`, `contact`), after the connection diagnostics.
- **What moves `topologyHash`:** a contact appearing or leaving (mounting, unmounting, deleting, plugging, unplugging), the earth node's first and last link, and **sliding a breaker to a stab of the other leg** (to a stab of the same leg, the netlist and the hash are unchanged, though the compiler's structure list records the slide). Contact resistances, earth and grounding resistances never do.

### 5. Element refs, order and encoding

- `ElementRef` gains `{ kind: 'contact', entity, relation: 'mount' | 'entry' | 'plug' | 'earth', contact }` (`entity` the dependent entity). This settles ADR 0020's follow-up: the contact id is never squeezed into an entity element's 32-character `part`, so `ContactId` keeps its grammar.
- Branches are entity elements, then connections, then contacts by (entity, relation code, contact id); each entity's contacts are mount, then plug, then earth.
- `topologyHash`: the branch tag **2** (varint entity, **u8 relation code**, str contact id), appended like an element-kind code; relation codes are hashed and append-only, **mount 0, entry 1, plug 2, earth 3**; the earth member encodes as entity 0. **`CNT` stays at version 1**: no world without contacts or an earth node encodes differently. `hash.spec.ts` and `contacts.spec.ts` pin a golden per new kind, per relation and for the earth node, and the byte layout of a contact branch.
- `netlist.dump` names a contact `e<entity>:<relation>.<contact>` (`e7:mount.line1`) and the earth member `0:earth`.

### 6. `wiringGraph`

`wiringGraph(world, catalog)` (exported from `sim-core`, for the inspector, PR 20) compiles the world's **wiring view** with the compiler itself (`NetlistCompilerOptions.view: 'wiring'`): each model's `wiring` topology (a switch closed; a fuse or breaker intact whatever its handle or trip), no sim state, every landed connection present whatever its ends' state, and every relation contact. Two terminals are `joined` when they are in one node or in nodes linked by connections, mount and plug contacts, the elements of kinds marked `wiringPath` (a protective device's line → load) and the parts a model lists in `wiringPathParts` (the service's drop neutral, so `x0` and `n` are joined), never through a load or a source, and **never through soil**: an `earth` contact joins nothing, so two rods with no conductor between them are not joined and a missing GEC or bonding jumper stays visible to the inspector (PR review P1). `earthed(ref)` says whether a terminal's wiring component holds an earth link. Properties tie it to `compileNetlist`: every pair of terminals in one electrical node, and both ends of every connection, mount and plug contact, are joined; an unconnected rod is joined to nothing else. Derived, never stored or hashed.

### 7. The oracle corpus

The AC corpus gains coupled pairs (ADR 0012's rule: a new stamp kind extends the corpus). In about half the AC circuits with two or more sources, the first two are coupled with a mutual resistance of either sign one decade below the smaller internal resistance (so the real block is positive definite) and a mutual inductance of either sign in the source-inductance range, drawn from a stream of their own (`oracle:ac:<i>:coupled`), so no other circuit's deck changed. The deck chains each coupled source through a **current-controlled voltage source** (`H`, R_m times the partner's sense current) and a **voltage-controlled source** (`ED`, L_m times the voltage of a 1 H inductor that an `F` source drives with the partner's current, i.e. jω I_p). Regenerated with `pnpm sim oracle regen` (ngspice 47, Sparse 1.3), decks and results together; every circuit agrees within ADR 0012's tolerance with the 10× margin.

- **`SIM_ENGINE_VERSION` 8**, provisional until v0.5.0: every split-phase line-to-neutral load moves (the T-equivalent), so every scenario with one is re-pinned once, listed old → new in the CHANGELOG; three-phase services and line-to-line loads don't move numerically. Later P4 PRs that change engine-8 behaviour re-pin in place.
- **`SIM_PROTOCOL_VERSION` 8**, provisional until v0.5.0: this PR brings only the descriptor's part (the `contact` element ref with its relation enum and a 255-character camelCase contact id, entity 0 allowed only as `earth`, and a contact ref as a trace's `branch` target), because a world with a contact can't otherwise cross the worker boundary. ## Decision (part 2): breakers v2, the GFCI and the AFCI (P4 PR 10)

### 9. The kinds and their keys (finalising ADR 0019's table)

Four new model kinds, each a placed entity with a `busStab` fit in content (PR 14) and one engine bank (`devices/protection/plug-on.ts`, the plan's "one breaker bank"). Terminals are fixed per kind:

| Kind               | Terminals                                | Sim keys                                                                                                             | Commanded keys |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| `breaker.plugOn1p` | `line`, `load`                           | `thermal`, `tripped`, `tripCause`, `magneticSinceTick`                                                               | `on`           |
| `breaker.plugOn2p` | `line1`, `line2`, `load1`, `load2`       | `thermalL1`, `thermalL2`, `tripped`, `tripCause`, `magneticSinceTick`                                                | `on`           |
| `breaker.gfci1p`   | `line`, `load`, `loadNeutral`, `pigtail` | `thermal`, `tripped`, `tripCause`, `magneticSinceTick`, `residualIntegral`, `residualSinceTick`, `testHeldSinceTick` | `on`, `test`   |
| `breaker.afci1p`   | `line`, `load`, `loadNeutral`, `pigtail` | `thermal`, `tripped`, `tripCause`, `magneticSinceTick`, `arcExposure`, `arcLastSeenTick`                             | `on`, `test`   |

Every key, type and initial value is ADR 0019's (no key added, renamed or reshaped): `tripCause` is the enum `thermal | magnetic | groundFault | arcFault | test` or `null` (`TRIP_CAUSES`, catalogue); the since-ticks are integers or `null`; `residualIntegral` and `arcExposure` are numbers in [0, 1]; `test` is a counter. The load check (world-model `tripCauseProblems`) refuses a `tripCause` that isn't `null` exactly when the device isn't tripped (and, for the receptacle kind to come, one held while it is latched). `save-format`'s `fixtures/v4/world/protection-state.json` already held exactly these key sets, so it is unchanged; a world-model spec now checks it against the final kinds.

**Parameters** (`PlugOnBreakerParams`, `GfciBreakerParams`, `AfciBreakerParams`, catalogue): the rating, the bimetal's τ and k_u (the P2 law; k_u strictly below 1.35, so 135 % always trips, UL 489 by number; the DIN kind keeps "at most 1.35"), an **absolute** pickup band in amperes (`magneticPickupMinA`/`MaxA`, refused at or below k_u I_n; no ratio field exists, so the DIN entry's 8–12 × band can't be carried over), the opening-time band (ms) and the pole's contact resistance; the GFCI adds the residual pickup band (A), the mechanism-time band (ms) and the test resistor (Ω). The AFCI's detection limits are the region's (`RegionProfile.protection.afci`), as the GFCI's inverse-time limit is (`protection.gfci`).

### 10. The netlist

Every pole is a resistor branch of `contactResistanceOhm` (`pole`, or `pole1` and `pole2`), present while the handle is on and the breaker hasn't tripped: one key, a common trip. The GFCI's and AFCI's neutral (`neutral`, `pigtail` → `loadNeutral`) is always present and never switched, so its current is known. The GFCI's held test closes a `test` resistor of `testResistanceOhm` from `load` to `pigtail`, around the neutral sensor, so the residual it draws is a real current. Sim topology keys: `tripped` (and the GFCI's `testHeldSinceTick`), each projected to a boolean by the compiler (below). The wiring view has every pole and the neutral, never the test resistor; all four kinds are `wiringPath`.

### 11. The thermal and magnetic elements (D6)

- **Thermal**, per pole: the P2 bimetal law, dθ/dt = ((I / I_n)² − θ) / τ, trip at θ ≥ k_u². With τ 60 s and k_u 1.25 it trips at 116.84 s at 135 %, 29.72 s at 200 %, 3.872 s at 500 % and 0.9449 s at 10 × (200 A on a 20 A breaker is below the band: thermal), inside the read bands of the pre-audit (135 %: 30 s–1 h; 200 %: 10 s–2 min; 500 %: 1–15 s; 10 ×: 0.45–1.1 s). It never trips at k_u I_n. An open pole cools.
- **Magnetic**, per pole: a pickup drawn once per bank build inside the absolute band (pole 1 from `entity:<id>`, P2's rule; pole 2 from `entity:<id>:pickupL2`). From the first protection step that sees any pole above its pickup (`magneticSinceTick`), the breaker opens after the opening time drawn from `entity:<id>:opening` as ⌈min + (max − min) u⌉ ticks (8–20 ms): the trip event is exactly that many ticks after the fault, and the fault current flows (and heats) until then. A dip below the pickup ends the excursion. A save inside the window opens at the same tick.
- **Common trip**: any pole's trip opens both. When two causes come at one step the first of magnetic, thermal, ground fault (or test), arc fault is reported.
- **Correction (content PR 14, `electrical-realism-reviewer`): above the pickup the magnetic path owns the trip.** As first shipped, a bimetal that crossed k_u² inside the opening window tripped at once, cause `thermal`: the law's time, about τ (k_u I_n / I)², falls under the drawn opening from about 1.03 kA on a 15 A pole (20 ms) and 1.62 kA (the 8 ms floor), so a bolted fault near a panel on the shipped service (about 1.6 kA line to neutral at the meter) could report `thermal` and open early. Physically the armature releases the latch within the first half-cycle and the mechanism sets the contact opening; the bimetal never initiates. Now, while any pole is above its pickup, no bimetal trips: the breaker opens at the drawn opening, cause `magnetic`. On a two-pole breaker this holds for the other pole's bimetal too (the trip unit is common and already released); if the excursion ends before the opening, a hot bimetal trips thermally at that step. Below the band's top a cold bimetal needs at least 93.5 ms (30 A at 950 A, the shipped minimum; 132 ms at 15 A and 400 A), so the thermal path needs no mechanism floor from cold; a bimetal preheated to its trip point by a long overload that crosses it below the pickup opens at that step (a labelled abstraction). No pinned hash moved: no pinned scenario reaches a bimetal trip inside a magnetic window.

### 12. The GFCI (D7)

`devices/protection/gfci.ts` (`GfciSensor`, shared with the receptacle): the residual |I_pole + I_neutral| each tick; a pickup drawn from `entity:<id>:gfciPickup` (4–6 mA) and a mechanism time from `entity:<id>:gfciMechanism` (⌈3–14⌉ ticks). The region's Class A limit is read **as a rate**, linear between its eight points (convex in I, so every chord lies inside the limit; linear in t overshoots by up to 22 %, which a spec shows); from the pickup to 6 mA the first point's rate (labelled); above 264 mA the rate is unbounded and the mechanism time alone decides. Σ dt / t(I) is persisted clamped at 1 and resets below the pickup. **The device opens at the last tick at or before the integral reaches 1 at the present rate**: at a constant residual max(t_m, ⌊t(I) / 1 ms⌋) ticks after the excursion began, never later than the region's tabulated limit (each point within 0.15 % of the published curve) and at most one tick early (10 mA: 2695 ticks; 100 mA: 100 ticks, inside 0.1001 s). A test press (the counter rising in the `commanded` hook) sets `testHeldSinceTick`; the held resistor's residual trips it through the normal path with cause `test` (15 kΩ on the test entry: 8.0 mA at 120 V, a rate of 0.17876 + 0.5 × 0.19230 = 0.27491 /s, about 3637 ticks), and the hold ends at the trip or after `GFCI_TEST_HOLD_TICKS` (5000: it covers the limit at the smallest realistic test current, a UL 943 test circuit of at most about 9 mA at rated voltage that must still trip at 85 % voltage; 15 kΩ at 102 V draws 6.8 mA, a 4.60 s limit). A trip on the step the hold lets go is still cause `test` (the hold over that step decides). Reset is the handle (off, then on), as for every breaker.

### 13. The AFCI (D8)

`devices/protection/afci.ts`. At each absolute 100-tick bucket boundary the AFCI judges the bucket that just ended (the state a conductor end was decided for, ADR 0018 §6): the largest current on a connection with an end `arcing` whose either node is **downstream** (reached from `load` and `loadNeutral` without crossing the breaker's own poles; computed lazily, only when the engine reports an arcing end, and cached per netlist). At I ≥ 5 A the bucket adds 1 / n to `arcExposure`, n the whole buckets inside the region's limit (at least one; one fewer at the strict "under 0.2 s" bound from 1.5 × rated), the limit interpolated linearly in rate between 1 s at 5 A and 0.2 s at 1.5 × rated (labelled interpolation). It trips if u < (x − x_prev) / (1 − x_prev), u = `intermittentDrawU32`(first output of `entity:<id>:afci`, bucket) / 2³², so the probability of no trip by a bucket is exactly 1 − x and the trip comes by the limit for every seed (a fast-check property). Below 5 A no bucket counts. The exposure is forgotten `AFCI_QUIET_RESET_TICKS` (1000) after `arcLastSeenTick`. A test press trips it at once (cause `test`, the event at the press's tick) when it is closed and its line-to-pigtail voltage in the last solution is at least `TRIP_ELECTRONICS_MIN_V` (60 V); otherwise it does nothing.

### 14. The engine

- **`SimDevice`** gains `parts` (each branch's part), the region's `protection` limits and `purposeStream(purpose)`.
- **The `commanded` hook** gains a `CommandedContext` (the tick, the last solution and its netlist, read-only, and an `emit` stamped at the command's tick), as ADR 0019 anticipated for the GFCI reset. Such an event can carry a replay start's end tick, so the sim worker discards a replay start's events by position, never by tick (`runner.ts`; ADR 0011 §4's commanded-tick note), and delivers what headless delivers.
- **`LayerContext`** gains `arcingCount()` and `arcingBranch(k)`: the netlisted connections with an arcing end, collected from the connection bank once per step on demand (`ConnectionThermalBank.arcingBranches`, no allocation).
- **The numeric structure code** (part 1's deviation 6): the compiler reads each sim topology key through a new `CompileOptions.simValue(entity, key)` (the bank's `simValue`, no bag built) and keeps one number per entity, bit k set when key k projects to `true` (`simTopologyFlag`: anything but `false`, `null` or missing), instead of a JSON string; models see the booleans. At most 30 keys per model. Measured on `intermittent-toggle-wired`: unchanged (0.780 ms median against 0.781 ms before, gated 0.976 against 0.977), since the wired plant's breakers and fuses already had a cheap view; the gain is for panels of GFCI and AFCI breakers, whose bags allocate.
- No new stamp kind (every new element is a resistor), so the oracle corpus is unchanged. No `topologyHash`, snapshot or world hash of an existing scenario moved; the new kinds have pinned `topologyHash` goldens (`hash.spec.ts`).

## Deviations from the plan and ADR 0019

1. **Protocol 8 starts in this PR** (ADR 0019's version table put it at PR 11). The descriptor must carry contact refs and the earth member as soon as the compiler makes them, or a world holding one fails the worker's descriptor parse. Only the descriptor's agreed shape lands here (ADR 0019 "Protocol 8": `ElementRef` gains `contact`, `TerminalRefSchema` allows entity 0 only with `earth`, a probe or trace target accepts a contact ref); PR 11 completes it in place. **For review by `renderer-engineer` and `angular-ui-engineer`** with the ADR 0011 amendment in PR 11.
2. **The service has a fourth terminal, `x0`** (the transformer's neutral bushing), so the utility's grounding sits where it does physically, before the drop neutral. Without it, the earth link would have to sit on the meter base's `n` and skip the drop neutral. `x0` is `access: 'none'` in content.
3. **The oracle deck uses controlled sources** (`H`, `ED` over an `F`-driven inductor), not "the T with negative R and L … else K-coupled inductors" (ADR 0019 §7): the corpus couples any two sources, not only two sharing a neutral, and a `K` coupling needs |L_m| ≤ √(L_a L_b). The controlled sources state R_m + jωL_m exactly, of either sign.
4. **The plan's "`inEntry` bonds" bullet** stays out (ADR 0019 deviation 7: conduit bonding is deferred past P4); the `entry` relation code is reserved.
5. **The lamp's `burnedOut` and `overvoltageSinceTick`** are still carried through unchanged (ADR 0019 PR 7 note 6 said engine 8 sets them). Setting them needs the `lampBurnout` and `overvoltage` events (protocol 8's events, PR 11) and the overvoltage dwell constant (F4.4); they move to the lamp work in F4.1 part 1 or F4.4 with those events. Engine 8 keeps the `overvoltageSinceTick` exemption in `scripting-api`'s `save.spec.ts` deliberately.
6. **The non-allocating numeric structure code** for topology keys (ADR 0019 "Sim and commanded state keys") is not in this PR: no engine-8 part 1 kind has a sim topology key, so it changes nothing measurable yet; it lands with the first P4 kinds that have them (PR 10's breakers and GFCIs), measured against `intermittent-toggle-wired` there.

**Part 2 (P4 PR 10):**

7. **No device records for the new kinds yet** (`output: null`): their protocol 8 records (per-pole currents, `residualMa`, `arcExposure`, the state and cause codes) are PR 11's, with the ADR 0011 amendment. Their behaviour is tested through bags, events and the solution. _Done in PR 11 ([ADR 0011's protocol 8 amendment](0011-sim-worker-contract.md#amendment-protocol-8-residential-records-and-events-p4)): the four breakers, the service and the ground rod publish records._
8. **A GFCI opens at the last tick at or before its limit**, not at the first tick after the integral reaches 1 (ADR 0019 wrote "Σ dt / t(I) ≥ 1"): the integral is still persisted that way, but the trip is taken one step ahead at the present rate, so at 100 mA the breaker is open at 100 ms, inside the 0.1001 s limit, where the literal rule gave 101 ms.
9. **The AFCI's T_limit is quantised to whole buckets** (D8 wrote Δt / T_limit(I)): each counted bucket adds 1 / n with n the whole 100 ms buckets inside the limit, so the bucket that takes the exposure to 1 is never after the limit; at the strict "under 0.2 s" point n is 1.
10. **The AFCI test button has no persisted key**: the press trips it in the `commanded` hook (when closed and powered), with the event at the press's tick; ADR 0019 listed only the counter, so nothing new is saved.
11. **`protection-state.json` is unchanged**: its fixture-only bags already held the final key sets; it keeps its fixture-only refs until the content PRs, and a world-model spec checks it against the four kinds.
12. **Not here:** the GFCI receptacle (F4.1 part 2, PR 13) and its `latched` logic, the `tick-protected-plant` bench case (pending, PR 26).

## Reference notes

Kept out of content `source` strings, which never name makers and carry no URLs.

- **The half-winding factors and the coupled block.** W. H. Kersting, _Distribution System Modeling and Analysis_ (CRC Press), the chapter on centre-tapped (single-phase three-wire) transformers; and W. H. Kersting, "Center-tapped transformer and 120/240-V secondary models", _IEEE Transactions on Industry Applications_, 2009. Kersting's approximation takes the half-winding impedances Z0 = 0.5 R + j0.8 X and Z1 = Z2 = R + j0.4 X (per unit of the full winding's R and X), which give the 1.5 R and 1.2 X line-to-neutral factors on the 120 V base and exactly the coupled block of §1.
- **A worked check.** EPRI's OpenDSS technical note "Modeling single-phase, center-tapped distribution transformers": its 25 kVA example (%R 0.6 / 1.2 / 1.2 on the three windings, X_HL = X_HT = 2.04 %, X_LT = 1.36 %) reproduces the 1.50 and 1.20 factors.
- **The utility's grounding range (1–25 Ω).** IEEE C2 (the National Electrical Safety Code) Rule 096, cited by number only: 25 Ω is its ceiling for a single made electrode; multigrounded utility neutrals sit lower. 5 Ω inside the range is a game-design choice.
- **Dwight's formula** is the one IEEE Std 142 gives for a single driven rod (cited by number).
- **Part 2: the plug-on curve.** The 240–400 A instantaneous band, the 8–20 ms opening top and the thermal bands are the P4 pre-audit's readings of one maker's published 15–20 A residential time-current curve (2026-11-17); the maker stays out of content, and PR 14's entries carry the reading as a paraphrase. UL 489 (the 1 h at 135 % and 2 min at 200 % ceilings), UL 943 (Class A) and UL 1699 (the combination AFCI's clearing times) are cited by number only.
- **Part 2: the Class A power law** t = (20 mA / I)^1.43 s is the published form behind the region profile's eight points (catalogue v3); `gfci.spec.ts` evaluates it with `Math.pow` to show the rate chords stay inside it. Sim code never evaluates it.
- **Part 2: UL 1699's intermediate levels.** UL 1699 also tests at intermediate levels (10 A and rated current); their limits are not verified here, and the linear-in-rate interpolation is labelled.
- **Part 2: the GFCI test circuit.** UL 943 (by number) bounds the test circuit at about 9 mA at rated voltage and requires it to trip at 85 % voltage; makers' test resistors are typically about 15 kΩ (about 8 mA). `GFCI_TEST_HOLD_TICKS` (5000) is sized from that (realism review R1, P4 PR 10).
- **Content PR 14 (F4.2 part 2): the documents read.** Kept here because content source strings never name makers.
  - The 30–50 A plug-on curves and the terminations page: Square D's _QO and QOB Miniature Circuit Breakers, Class 730_ catalogue, dated 08/98 (PDF created 1998), its characteristic trip curves 730-4 (15–25 A), 730-5 (30–40 A) and 730-6 (50–70 A), read from the drawings at 300 dpi (log-axis readings, about ±15 %), and its terminations table. Its arc-fault breakers (QO-AFI) are a first generation from before UL 1699 (1999) and the combination type; the AFCI entries' citation says their terminal class is assumed for the combination AFCI.
  - The 20 A AFCI's resistive loss (about 2.2 W): Schneider Electric data bulletin 0600DB1603, _Circuit Breaker Operating Temperatures_ (read in full).
  - The DIN miniature-breaker watts-loss figures: Hager's general catalogue 2017–18 (read through a page fetch, columns matched by position).
  - The 2 AWG aluminium XHHW-2 construction and its 20 °C cross-check: Southwire's SIMpull XHHW-2 aluminium specification 10014 (read in full; its ampacity column not used).
  - The meter-socket lug ranges and dimensions: Milbank's meter mounting equipment catalogue for the North and South Carolina utilities (read).
  - Not read (search summaries only): triplex drop makers' listings, meter-lug torque figures and electricians' GFCI test-resistor measurements; each is labelled so in its entry.
