# Rendering caveats

**Load this when:** working in `libs/rendering/renderer` (scene, world sync, cameras, picking, selection, the placement ghost and gestures, overlays, the wire renderer and preview rope, the terminal and wire pickers and the routing gesture, the frame loop), the snap maths in `libs/domain/world-model/src/lib/snap`, or the Angular viewport host, or adding a Three.js-side dependency. Background: [rendering architecture](../architecture/rendering.md), plan §4.9. WebGPU/WebGL2 and headless-browser notes are in the [Electron caveats](electron.md).

- **The renderer draws the world; it never owns or mutates it.** Hover, selection, the ghost, mode and camera pose are view state. Gestures leave the controller as `ViewportIntent`s (`place`, `move`, `cancelPlacement`) and the `Viewport` component executes them through `Session.exec`. Never give the renderer a `Session` or an `exec` callback. Selection is not saved and not logged; a Command that acts on it takes the entity id as an argument (the Delete binding reads `selection.entityId`).
- **Two layers: `ViewportController` (no GPU) and `ViewportRenderer` (GPU shell).** All scene, camera, input and picking logic sits in the controller so it can be unit-tested in Node with fake DOM targets (`viewport-controller.spec.ts`). Don't put logic in `ViewportRenderer` or the Angular component; it can't be tested there.
- **Pure modules are lint-fenced.** `cameras/camera-math`, `input/input-actions`, `picking/selection`, `stats/frame-timer`, `sim/display-curve` and `sim/lamp-display` may not import `three`, `three-mesh-bvh`, the viewport or the picker, and may not touch `Math.random`, `Date.now`, `performance`, `window` or `document` (`libs/rendering/renderer/eslint.config.mjs`). This keeps them GPU-free and deterministic.
- **Snap and placement maths live in `@controls/world-model` (`snap/`), not the renderer** (moved in F1.7), so scripts and `sim-cli` snap identically to the mouse. Domain rules apply there: 90 % coverage and the determinism lint. The renderer only turns a ray hit into integer mm and calls `placeOnTargets`; put any new placement rule in `world-model`, or the mouse and scripts will disagree.
- **Snap inputs and outputs are integer millimetres, anchored at the footprint's lower-left corner.** Centres of odd-sized footprints would be half-millimetres, so the anchor is the min corner. Convert a raycast hit with `metresToMm` (rounds half away from zero, never `-0`) before calling `snap()`; it throws on non-integers. A device centred on a DIN rail sits at `v = -floor(h / 2)`, so odd heights are biased 0.5 mm down. That's deliberate and deterministic.
- **Stud positions are `round(first + i × 406.4)`**, never a running sum of a rounded 406 mm step (which drifts 5 mm over 12 studs). Keep published values exact in `snap/constants.ts` and round once at the end.
- **Every snap constant has a `source`, and `kind: 'game-design'` values say so in the text** (realism charter, plan §3). A spec checks this. `electrical-realism-reviewer` re-checks them in P2.
- **Surface frames are axis-aligned in P1** (`normal: '+x' | '-x' | '+z' | '-z'`, `u = up × normal`). A mounted entity's orientation is the ADR 0007 triple; `orientationForNormal` maps each normal to a yaw quarter-turn (`+z` 0, `+x` 90 000, `-z` 180 000, `-x` 270 000 mdeg). Don't invent a second rotation type.
- **An entity's `positionMm` is the snap anchor:** the footprint's lower-left corner on the mounting face. Prop geometry is built with its origin at its min corner and spans +x (width), +y (height), +z (depth). Change one side and the other must follow, or placed props float off their snap point.
- **Scene units are metres and world units are integer mm and millidegrees.** Convert only with `mm()` / `metresToMm()` / `mdegToRadians()` from `@controls/units`, and only at draw time. Euler order `YXZ` is ADR 0007's intrinsic yaw, pitch, roll.
- **Picking is two-level BVH, never a whole-scene raycast.** An `ObjectBVH` over the selectable roots narrows the ray, then each candidate mesh raycasts through its geometry's `MeshBVH` (`acceleratedRaycast`). At 500 synthetic devices: about 2.4 µs per pick and 28 ms to build (Node, F1.7).
  - BVHs are built with `{ indirect: true }`, so the geometry's index buffer, already uploaded to the GPU, is never reordered.
  - `Mesh.prototype.raycast` is **not** patched globally. Only registered meshes get `acceleratedRaycast` as an own property, and `Picker.dispose()` deletes it.
  - `ObjectBVH` can't be built over zero objects (it takes `log2` of the count), so the picker skips it when empty.
  - `ObjectBVH.raycast` allocates a few small objects per call. Hover raycasts therefore run at most once per frame, and only when the pointer, the camera or the walk pose changed (`hoverDirty`).
  - After moving selectables, call `Picker.refit()`; after adding or removing them, call `setRoots()`. World sync drives both. `setRoots()` also drops the BVH and the own `raycast` of any mesh no longer under a root, so removing entities returns memory to baseline.
  - **Placement aims with a second picker over snap surfaces only.** The main picker returns the nearest hit (`firstHitOnly`), so a prop in front of the plate would block the ghost. Both pickers share geometry BVHs: whichever builds one owns it, and both are disposed.
- **Selectables are marked with `userData.selectable`** (JSON-plain `SelectableInfo`), so the scene-graph snapshot shows them. Nested selectables belong to their outermost marked ancestor. Keyboard cycling order is code-unit id order (`<`), never `localeCompare`, so it's the same on every machine.
- **Input actions are not Commands.** WASD, mouse look, selection cycling and the placement keys (`commitPlacement` Enter, `moveSelection` M) go through `input/input-actions.ts` (a rebindable `KeyboardEvent.code` map), so per-frame input never enters the replay log (ADR 0006); F1.4 feeds user bindings in via `setBindings()`. Since F1.7, switching mode (`V`) and focusing (`F`) are the `view.mode` / `view.focus` Commands, bound by the keybinding service; the renderer must leave those keys alone so they reach it. Chords with Ctrl, Meta or Alt are ignored here because they belong to Commands.
- **Gestures commit once.** Only a click, `Enter`, or releasing a drag produces an intent, and at most one. Pointer moves only re-aim the ghost (at most one surface pick per frame, through `hoverDirty`). A drop at the entity's current position, or on no valid snap, produces nothing. A move keeps the entity's orientation, so only same-facing targets count: one `entity.move`, never a rotate as well.
- **Our pointer listeners are registered before `OrbitControls`, in the capture phase.** A press on an entity sets `orbit.enabled = false` before OrbitControls' own `pointerdown` runs, so that drag moves the entity instead of orbiting; `pointerup` re-enables it. Constructing OrbitControls first breaks drag-to-move.
- **The ghost borrows recipe geometry and gives it back.** It never keeps a pointer to a released geometry (it swaps in its own empty placeholder), so it has no "disposed twice" or "rendered after dispose" path. Prop recipes are reference-counted across entities and the ghost; `recipeCount` reaching 0 is the baseline check.
- **Keyboard input listens on the canvas only.** The canvas has `tabindex="0"` and is focused on pointer-down, so typing in a text field never moves the camera. Held keys are cleared on canvas or window `blur`, otherwise a key released elsewhere stays stuck down.
- **Pointer lock:** `requestPointerLock()` must run inside a user gesture (a click in walk mode). The browser releases it on Esc by itself; we only listen for `pointerlockchange`. Chromium returns a promise that rejects when the lock is denied, so the rejection is swallowed. **E2E never asserts pointer lock** (flaky headless). Drive modes through the `?e2e=1` hook and assert on the host's `data-*` attributes.
- **The `?e2e=1` hook** (`window.__controlsViewport`) exposes the `ViewportRenderer` API (`setMode`, `focus`, `select`, `selectNext`, `pickAt`, `projectMm`, `cameraPositionMm`, `stats`, …). It is view state only and can't touch the world. It's gated by the query param, not by dev mode, because CI runs E2E against the production build. E2E aims the mouse with `projectMm` (integer-mm world point → canvas pixels) and lets snapping absorb the rounding.
- **E2E must wait for the renderer to draw a world change before using the hook on it.** A console line, palette run or key runs its command synchronously, but the viewport draws `Session.world()` from an Angular effect (`renderer.syncWorld`), which runs at the next change detection: zoneless Angular schedules that as a task (a `setTimeout` and animation-frame race), and on a busy main thread (software WebGL2 on a CI runner) several protocol round trips can run first. Until then `select('entity-2')`, `pickAt` and `sceneGraph()` see the old world (`select` returns false). Wait on something the same change detection renders, such as the line's own console output (`runLogged` in `apps/game-e2e/src/support/console.ts` matches `: ok (log #n)`; a bare `/: ok/` matches the previous line's output and waits for nothing), or poll the renderer itself (`expectDrawn`, on `selectables()`). Seen on a macOS runner (PR #80): `bench-lamp.spec.ts` selected the switch before it was drawn, on all three attempts.
- **E2E must wait for placement to be armed before clicking.** Pressing a tool-belt key arms placement through an Angular effect, a tick later. Wait for `data-placement-ref` to name the expected prop (`data-placement-valid` may still be `true` from the previous prop), or the click can place the wrong prop or select instead.
- **OrbitControls in work mode:** the pan limit is `cursor` + `maxTargetRadius` (the focus sphere), and orbit is clamped to ±75° azimuth and ±60° elevation around the face normal. Damping is switched off for the `update()` that applies a new focus, or momentum from the old target carries over. A press and release within 4 px (`CLICK_SLOP_PX`) is a click (select); anything more is an orbit drag.
- **Camera rotation order is `YXZ`** (yaw, then pitch) for walk mode. `OrbitControls` writes the quaternion directly, so switching modes is safe.
- **Disposal** goes through a LIFO `Disposer`. The controller registers the scene first, so it's disposed last, after the overlays and BVHs that reference it. `viewport-controller.spec.ts` proves that after `dispose()` no listeners are left on the canvas, document or window, every geometry and material is disposed exactly once, no BVH or own `raycast` remains (overlays keep their deliberate no-op `raycast`), and pointer lock is released; a second test does the same with world entities and an armed ghost. `WorldSync` and the ghost take their meshes out of the scene before `disposeScene` runs, because their geometry belongs to the shared recipes, not the scene: a recipe disposed by both would fail the "exactly once" check. `viewport-renderer.spec.ts` does the same for the GPU shell with a mocked `WebGPURenderer`: no listener, pending frame or idle-stats timer survives `dispose()`. Any new resource owner must register with the disposer and be added to those tests.
- **The viewport renders on demand: a change nobody reports is never drawn.** `RenderLoop` (`loop/render-loop.ts`) requests an animation frame only when asked, and none while idle (behind the main menu it draws once, then nothing). Requests come from the controller's `invalidate()`, which is already called by `markHoverDirty()`, `emit()`, held-key changes, mouse look and OrbitControls' `change`; and from `ViewportRenderer` (first frame, resize, `webglcontextrestored`, `visibilitychange`). **If you add something that changes what is drawn without going through those** (a new overlay that animates, a material or light tweak, a texture that finishes loading, a sim-snapshot-driven effect from P2), call `controller.invalidate()` or `ViewportRenderer.requestRender()`; if it animates, return `true` from `update()` until it settles. Forget it and the screen shows the old frame until the next mouse move, and e2e that waits on the change times out. Never "fix" that by going back to a continuous loop. Inside `update()`, `invalidate()` is ignored (the frame in progress draws the change); return `true` to get another frame.
  - **Tests wait on the loop, never on time.** Unit tests drive `RenderLoop` with `ManualFrameClock`. E2E uses the hook's `whenIdle()` (everything requested is on screen; never resolves while a key is held) and `afterFrames(n)` (draws n frames, requesting them). A browser `requestAnimationFrame` no longer implies a render.
  - **three r186 runs its own rAF loop from `Renderer.init()`** even without `setAnimationLoop`. `ViewportRenderer` stops it through the private `_animation.stop()` (guarded: if a release renames it, drawing stays on demand but the page wakes every vsync again; check after a three upgrade) and calls `info.reset()` before each draw, since nothing else resets the counters now.
  - **Resize and thumbnails draw synchronously** (`renderNow()`): resizing clears the canvas, and the thumbnail must copy the drawing buffer in the task that drew it.
- **Frame-time HUD:** dev builds, or `?hud` in any build. It shows each frame's **main-thread cost** (`update` + draw submission), mean and worst per 500 ms window (not an EMA: the first frame includes shader compilation and would skew an average for seconds), and an fps figure only over back-to-back frames (walking, orbit damping). When nothing was drawn in a window it reads `idle · last frame N ms`. The cost excludes GPU-process time: under SwiftShader a walk showed 0.7 ms a frame at 39 fps. Calling `stats()` from the hook also starts a new window.
- **Headless WebGPU (`--enable-unsafe-webgpu --use-webgpu-adapter=swiftshader`) is unreliable here.** On Fedora 44 with Playwright's Chromium the device logs `Instance dropped in popErrorScope` at start-up; after New sandbox, buffer creation fails, and `captureThumbnail()` returns a blank image even on the menu. This is the same before and after on-demand rendering (checked in October 2026). CI exercises WebGL2; check WebGPU on a real GPU.
- **Headless frame times aren't representative.** Software WebGL2 (SwiftShader) at 1920×1080 rendered the bare panel at about 3 fps in F1.7 part 1, while WebGPU-on-SwiftShader-Vulkan held 60 fps. With 500 props (258 draw calls after culling) it flips: WebGL2 holds 60 fps and WebGPU-on-SwiftShader drops to about 3.8 fps, because each WebGPU draw is expensive on the CPU. The same 500 props hold 60 fps on both backends on a real GPU (RX 7600). Don't tune against headless numbers; measure the 500-device budget on real integrated GPUs, and consider instancing repeated props if WebGPU per-draw cost shows up there too.
- **Run E2E against your own server when other worktrees are active.** `playwright.config.mts` reuses whatever is listening on port 4200 (`reuseExistingServer`), which may be another checkout's build. Start `nx run game:serve:development --port <free>` and point a config with `webServer` unset and `baseURL` set at it. Under heavy machine load, "waiting for element to be stable" timeouts are load, not regressions: rerun with `--workers=1`. The dev server live-reloads on any source change, which navigates the page: a long manual run (the frame-time tools build their plant for minutes) dies with "Execution context was destroyed" if you edit a source file meanwhile.
- **The scene is the world's fixtures (F1.9).** `buildBaseScene()` holds only lights; `FixtureSync` draws `world.fixtures` (one group per fixture, named by its id, with ref-counted recipes from `geometry/fixtures`) and `WorldSync` draws entities. Never add level geometry to the scene directly: put it in the level document, or scripts and the hash won't know about it. A fixture's id is its selectable id, `view.focus` target and snap-target id.
- **Snap targets come from `worldSnapTargets(world)`**, not from the scene's `userData`. The selectables still carry `snapTarget` (for the scene-graph snapshot and tests), and a spec checks the two agree.
- **Raycasts skip invisible objects.** three-mesh-bvh's `ObjectBVH.raycast` ignores `object.visible === false`, so the stud wall's snap plane (the ray must find the wall between studs) is a visible mesh whose **material** has `visible: false`: picked, never drawn. The scene-graph snapshot marks it `drawn: false`.
- **The placement picker also holds fixtures without a snap target** (the backboard), so they hide the surfaces behind them. A hit on the backboard is on no target and the ghost hides; without it, a click on the board would place a box on the studs behind it.
- **The initial focus is the first backplate**, else the first focusable (not simply the first selectable in id order: `backboard` sorts before `backplate`). It is applied when the first world with fixtures arrives, and again if the focused fixture disappears. `resetView()` (on a new session, `Session.generation`) returns to it in work mode.
- **Selection cycles in code-unit id order**, so on the workshop: `backboard`, `backplate`, `bench`, `din-rail-1`, `din-rail-2`, `entity-1…`, `stud-wall` (`entity-` sorts before `stud-wall`). E2E that cycles with `]` must count from that list.
- **Bench tops are horizontal frames** (`normal: '+y'`, `right` along the front edge, `v` from the front edge to the back). A prop set on one is turned by `orientationForFrame`: yaw for `right`, then pitch 270 000 mdeg (-90°), so its depth stands up and its footprint (width × height) lies on the top. A move keeps orientation, so a wall-mounted prop can't be dragged onto a bench in one `entity.move` (`placeOnTargets` filters targets by `orientationForFrame`).
- **DIN slots start at `round(k × 17.5)` mm** (DIN 43880 module width, rounded once, F1.9), not at multiples of an integer pitch. Use `dinSlotStartMm(k)`; never step by 18 (the pre-F1.9 rounded pitch, which drifted 0.5 mm per module).
- **The workshop screenshot baseline** (`apps/game-e2e/src/workshop.spec.ts`, `workshop.spec.ts-snapshots/workshop-chromium-linux.png`, owned by `test-engineer`) is stable because everything that varies is pinned: seed 0, six props placed through the session hook at fixed positions, `?renderer=webgl2`, Chromium's SwiftShader (`--use-angle=swiftshader`, so a developer GPU and a GPU-less runner rasterise alike), a 1280 × 720 viewport at DPR 1, `view.focus stud-wall`, the pointer off the canvas, and a stylesheet that hides the HUDs, crosshair and focus ring. Tolerance: threshold 0.2 per pixel, at most 1 % of pixels. It runs on Linux only (other OSes skip). **To update it** after an intended visual change: `pnpm nx run game-e2e:e2e -- workshop.spec.ts --update-snapshots` on Linux, look at the PNG, commit it with the change. It passes against both the dev server and the production build CI serves.
- **Hide overlays with `page.addStyleTag`, not the screenshot `style` option.** With Playwright 1.64 and this app, `toHaveScreenshot({ style })` left the HUD and focus ring in the captured image; a style tag added before the assertion works. Don't use `mask` for the dev HUD: it exists only in dev builds and its width changes with its numbers.
- **Don't use `addUpdateRange()` on a large instanced attribute (three r186).** In the F1.10 spike, a partial upload of the `instanceMatrix` of a large `InstancedMesh` (102 400 instances) made every later frame cost 40 to 250 ms of CPU on both WebGPU and WebGL2; a full upload (`needsUpdate = true`, no ranges) cost nothing measurable. Re-upload the whole buffer, or split into smaller meshes, until a three.js release fixes it. Details: [ADR 0010](../decisions/0010-wire-routing-spike.md).
- **Catalogue devices with a recipe draw from their catalogue entry** (F2.4, F2.5, `geometry/devices.ts`): the battery, the bench supply, the DIN PSU, the SPST switch, the lamp, the resistor and the heater. Size and terminal positions come from `content/devices/`, the same numbers the simulator and leads use; only the look is the renderer's. Bench devices put their top at z = depth and their front at y = 0 (they're laid on a bench top pitched −90°); the DIN PSU faces +z. The F2.5 "on a base" devices stand up their depth from z = 0, with a screw head on the base top at each terminal. A catalogue kind without a recipe draws as the unknown cube. Since F3.5 part 1 the breaker (a DIN-rail case facing +z, its terminal screws in the top and bottom faces, its handle a lever turned by `state.on`), the fuse and the enclosures have recipes too.
- **A lamp that isn't `solved` is never drawn as an off lamp** (the validity rule, `sim/lamp-display.ts`; ADR 0011 §9.6). `solved`: drawn at its display level, and level 0 (clear glass, no glow) is a real "off". `unsettled`: drawn at its level with a violet-grey bulb tint. `unsolved` (unmodelled, AC pending, mixed), `singular`, or no entry in the map given to `setLampOutputs` (before the first snapshot, after a source reset): **unknown**, with no glow and a blue-grey tint. An empty map therefore shows every lamp as unknown, not dark. The scene-graph snapshot records `lamp: { level, status }` with status `solved | unknown | unsettled`. Don't "simplify" an unknown lamp to level 0 solved; that's the "dark = 0 W" bug (#52 review).
- **The lamp display curve is a game-design choice, not photometry** (`sim/display-curve.ts`, pure and lint-fenced): `level = round(255 × (1 − e^(−ln5·L)))`, 0 below `L = 1/255`. Rated light is level 204 (`LAMP_RATED_LEVEL`); over-voltage is brighter and saturates toward 255. Change it there only, and update the snapshots on purpose.
- **Lamp outputs redraw only when a drawn step moves.** `setLampOutputs` compares each lamp's quantised level and status with what it drew, and calls `invalidate()` only on a change; a step writes only uniforms (bulb colour and `emissiveIntensity`), never `visible` or defines. The half-step hysteresis ADR 0011 §9.2 mentions is **not** applied to lamps: a lamp settling exponentially toward its steady value would park one step short of it for ever (it never crosses the extra half step), so its drawn level would depend on history. Plain rounding is stateless, and sim output settles, so boundary flicker lasts a frame or two at most.
- **Each lamp entity has its own bulb material**, a clone of the recipe's `bulb` slot (`BULB_SLOT`); the other slots stay the shared recipe materials. `WorldSync.remove` disposes the clone, and the recipe disposes its own bulb with the recipe. The ghost never sees a clone (it draws the recipe geometry with its single material).
- **A switch's lever is a child mesh** (`lever`, shared geometry owned by the recipe, the recipe's `accent` material). It turns ±25° about x from the entity's **commanded** `state.closed` on each sync of a new entity snapshot, so a `device.operate` shows at once; it's world state, not sim output. Its geometry gets a BVH like any mesh under a selectable root, and a pick on it selects the switch.
- **One mesh per device, a material per geometry group.** A device recipe is one merged geometry with a group per material slot (`DEVICE_SLOTS`) and a material array, so a device is one pick, one BVH and one recipe in `PropRecipes`, which disposes every material on the last release. The placement ghost draws the same geometry with its single material, which three draws over every group; don't give the ghost a material array.
- **A device's label is data, not a texture.** The fictional brand and model (`Ferrovane FV12-7`) is on the mesh's `userData.label` and in the scene-graph snapshot (`label`); the label plate is plain geometry. No canvas text: it needs the DOM, and the controller is tested in Node.
- **Catalogue devices are `device` selectables**, props are `prop`. Since F3.5 the mouse and the reticle place catalogue devices as well as props, through `placeInWorld` (world-model `snap/world-placement.ts`, which supersedes `placeOnTargets` for the viewport; scripts may still call either). A device's footprint is its body's width and height; it snaps to a fixture DIN rail only if it fits `din35`.
- **Placement aims at enclosure mounts first** (F3.5): `placeInWorld` reads mount targets from the world (`worldMountTargets`), not from the scene, and the surface picker holds the drawn enclosures (`WorldSync.enclosureRoots()`) so the ray finds a rail or backplate inside a panel. When membership changes, `syncWorld` rebuilds both pickers (`refreshSelectables`, then `refreshSurfaces`); when transforms change it refits both. Forget the surface picker and a moved panel's rails are aimed at where the panel was.
- **A valid ghost always commits, and an invalid one says why.** `placeInWorld` runs the command's own checks (`checkMount`, `entityHold`) and the overlap check in the stricter `all` scope (the commands use `mounting`: see the [commands caveats](commands.md)), and returns the refusal code as `reason`. The `Viewport` shows a polite status line for the rule-breaking reasons (`mount-full`, `overlaps`, `occupied`, `wired`, `off-mount`, `too-large`) and publishes `data-placement-reason`. Gestures and keys run through `Session.execFromInput`, so a refusal that still happens (Delete on an occupied enclosure) becomes a warning toast; the console and palette report their own errors and never raise one.
- **The mount origin is the mounted device's lower-left back corner** (ADR 0015 implementation note 7), so a rail mount's `positionMm.y` is where a device's bottom edge goes, not the rail centreline. The DIN panel puts it 45 mm below the centreline (half of DIN 43880's 90 mm), and its recipe draws the rail there (`DIN_MOUNT_CENTRE_MM`). A 125 mm device sits off-centre on its rail. A 40 mm breaker isn't a whole number of 17.5 mm modules, so two side by side leave a gap (slot 7 still overlaps slot 5's breaker; slot 8 is free).
- **Enclosure entries are two meshes per enclosure, never one per entry** (F3.5): `entries:closed` and `entries:open`, children of the entity mesh, from the commanded `openEntries` bits. Per-entry meshes cost up to ten extra draws per box (the 500-device budget). The all-closed geometry is the recipe's (`entriesAll`, shared); an entity with open entries owns its two merged geometries, rebuilt only when the bits change and disposed then and on removal. They are `userData.unpickable` (the picker skips them: no BVH, no patched `raycast`) with their own no-op `raycast`, so a pick lands on the body. The scene-graph snapshot shows `openEntries` on the entity node, not per entry.
- **Enclosure bodies are open-fronted and double-sided** (`side: DoubleSide` on the body slot), so the inside reads through the open front. Shape and wall thickness are a renderer look keyed by catalogue id (`ENCLOSURE_STYLES`: the round ceiling box, the blue nonmetallic boxes); anything unlisted is a rectangular steel box with 2 mm walls. A new enclosure entry with another shape needs a row there.
- **The DIN rail geometry is extruded and non-indexed**; index it (`mergeVertices`) before merging it with the indexed box parts of a recipe, or `mergeGeometries` returns `null` ("device geometry could not be merged").
- **The debug overlays draw their own leads and terminals** (F2.6, `overlays/sim-overlays.ts`). ADR 0011 §9.2 has overlays recolour "the existing lead and terminal meshes", but nothing draws leads until P3's wire routing, and device recipes don't mark terminals, so the overlays draw a straight two-half segment per connection and a marker per catalogue terminal (`positionMm` turned by the entity's transform). When P3 draws real conductors, recolour those instead and drop the overlay's own lead layer. A lead whose end is on a device the catalogue doesn't know isn't drawn.
- **Band 0 is "no scale", and a band needs a row to get one.** `scalesByBand(NOMINAL_BANDS)` resolves each band index to a voltage scale by **name** (`dc12`, `dc24`, `ac120-240`, `ac208y120`, `ac480y277` in `sim/overlay-scale.ts`); index 0 and any name without a row resolve to `null`, drawn light grey with "No nominal band" in the legend, never as bin 0. A spec fails if `NOMINAL_BANDS` gains a name the table lacks: when sim-core starts choosing bands, use these names or add rows. Until then every island is band 0, so the voltage overlay shows no colours on solved circuits; only the current overlay has a scale for every band.
- **Unknown is not zero.** Steps start _unknown_ (`STEP_UNKNOWN`), and stay so for an island that isn't `solved` (singular, non-converged, unmodelled, mixed, 0 iterations), a solved island with no reference node (voltage only), an item missing from the current generation (a lead connected since the last descriptor) and after a source reset (`null`). A solved 0 V or 0 A is bin 0, a real colour. Unknown draws dark grey, thinner, with diamond terminal markers, so it isn't told apart by colour alone; the legend says it in words.
- **AC current has no direction in the overlay (a documented abstraction).** A phasor's sign is a phase, not a flow, so AC (and mixed) leads get swaying diamonds whose rate follows |I|; only DC gets arrows, pointing by the sign of the real part (positive from the connection's `a` to `b`). Arrow speed shows magnitude bins (20–200 mm/s), not drift velocity.
- **Current animates only while the sim runs.** `SimOverlays.update()` returns `true` (another frame) only while `SnapshotFeed.running` (`SimHost.running`), the current overlay is on, reduced motion is off and some lead is above bin 0; otherwise the arrows go back to rest and the loop idles. Reduced motion is settings `display.reducedMotion` (`reduce`), or `prefers-reduced-motion` while it is `system` (`connectViewportOverlays`). Tests drive it with a fake feed's `running` flag, never with time.
- **The overlays read every snapshot, drawn or not,** so switching one on while the sim is paused (no new snapshot) still shows the latest values. The per-snapshot path is `NetlistReadout.read` (allocation-free, `sim bench` `overlay-read-500-devices`), then `updateOverlaySteps` into preallocated arrays; colours and matrices are rewritten only when a step changed, and only then is a frame requested. A new descriptor resets every step to unknown first (`resetOverlaySteps`), so hysteresis never keeps a bin from the old numbering or band. **That whole per-snapshot path, and a frame of `update()`, are gated at under 1 B per call in `sim-overlays.spec.ts`** (the `sim bench` method, copied: `sim-cli` may import domain libs only, so its `overlay-read-500-devices` case covers the `NetlistReadout` part). Measure with the listener itself: a `Set.forEach` callback in a test feed allocates on its own.
- **Overlay meshes stay out of the scene while nothing is shown,** so every existing scene-graph snapshot (and the workshop screenshot) is unchanged with the overlays off. Their names start `overlay:sim`, which the controller disposal spec's own-`raycast` exemption relies on. Instance buffers grow by doubling into a new `InstancedMesh` (`InstancedPool`), never resized in place; the old mesh is disposed **with its own geometry**: see the next bullet.
- **Overlay e2e reads the overlay graph, not pixels.** `window.__controlsViewport.overlayGraph()` gives each terminal's and lead's step (`noScale`, `unknown` or a bin), the direction and arrow count; poll it with `expect.poll` until the worker's snapshot has arrived. Don't wait on `whenIdle()` with the current overlay on and the sim running: the arrows animate, so the loop is never idle and it never resolves (use `afterFrames(n)` to measure frames).
- **A cut-to-length part's recipe key includes its length** (F3.5 part 2): always go through `PropRecipes.keyOf(ref, state)`, `acquire(ref, state)` and `release(ref, state)` with the **same** state the part was drawn with; releasing with another length leaks one recipe and frees nobody's. `WorldSync` keeps the key it drew (`recipeKey`) and compares keys, not refs, so a `raceway.cut` rebuilds the pickers. Never scale a unit duct instead: the slot comb would stretch.
- **Conduit parts aim at raceways, so the surface picker holds them** (`WorldSync.surfaceRoots()`: enclosures and raceways). Forget them and the cursor can never be near a stick's end except through a fixture behind it.
- **A connector's throat is drawn outside its body box on purpose** (inside the enclosure wall): bounds checks on recipes must not assume geometry stays within `sizeMm` for a connector, and the overlap body is only the part outside the wall.
- **`turnPlacement` (R) is view state.** The turn ends up in the logged `entity.place` orientation, never in the log by itself; it is only consumed while placing (with nothing armed the key falls through) and is kept from one placement to the next.
- **Route maths and the bake live in `@controls/world-model` (`routing/`), not the renderer** (F3.2 part 1, [ADR 0017](../decisions/0017-routing-and-bake.md)), like the snap maths: `autoRoute`, `snapWaypoint`, `assignLanes`, `bakeWorldRoutes`. The renderer (part 2) draws `bakeWorldRoutes`' polylines and never computes a route shape of its own; the live Verlet rope is the only renderer-side shape, and it is never committed, stored or hashed. A gesture commits the router's explicit waypoints and a length, through `wire.add`.
- **`routing/` is under the D6 lint and must stay there:** `+ − × ÷`, `Math.sqrt`, rounding, and literal constants (the elbow's arc uses a table of 15° cosines as literals, never `Math.cos`). A helper in a sibling directory isn't covered by the lint, so routing maths can't move out of `routing/`. Keep the order of floating-point operations fixed: the centreline golden is the same in Node and Chromium only because both evaluate the same operations in the same order.
- **The baked centreline is a picture.** Lead-outs, lanes, sag and coils are never checked by `route-collides` and never reach `lengthMm`; the command checks only the stored polyline (terminal points and waypoints). Don't make a bake setting (sag cap, lane pitch, lead-out) decide anything a command does, or a picture tweak would change what logs replay to. The parabolic sag is itself a drawing abstraction: solid 14–12 AWG in a real panel holds a bent shape rather than hanging in a curve.
- **Coordinates in a bake are integer tenths of a millimetre** (`BAKE_UNITS_PER_MM`). Convert to scene metres in the renderer's one place (`mm()` takes millimetres: divide by 10 first).
- **The routing goldens are generated, not hand-edited:** `libs/domain/world-model/goldens/routing/routing-panel.{world,digests}.json` (prettier-ignored) come from `routing/parity.spec.ts` over `routingParityWorld` (ducts, a riser, a coil, a conduit run with an elbow, a stud wall), which asserts each feature is exercised; regenerate with `pnpm nx test world-model -u` after a routing or bake change, and `apps/game-e2e/src/routing-parity.spec.ts` then checks Chromium against them. A digest change is expected for a deliberate maths change, never for a refactor.
- **Disposing an `InstancedMesh` does not free its instance buffers in three r186; disposing its geometry does.** `InstancedMesh.dispose()` releases the render object's bindings, which is all a small mesh's `instanceMatrix` uses (a uniform buffer). Above the uniform-buffer limit (64 KiB: about 1 024 instances) three draws the instances from interleaved vertex attributes instead (`nodes/accessors/Instance.js`), and those, with `instanceColor`, are deleted only by the geometry's dispose handler (`Geometries.initGeometry`), which knows just the first render object the geometry was drawn with. A geometry shared by successive meshes therefore leaked every replaced mesh's buffers (F3.2 part 2 review). So **every `InstancedPool` mesh owns its geometry** (`makeGeometry`), disposed with it; the material stays shared. Never share one geometry between instanced meshes that come and go. `apps/game-e2e/src/wires.spec.ts` checks `renderer.info.memory` attributes return to baseline after a 1 000-plus-segment wire is deleted, twice (the hook's `memory()`).
- **The wire renderer draws `bakeWorldRoutes` and nothing else** (F3.2 part 2, `wires/wire-renderer.ts`). Don't add route, lane or sag maths to it; a shape that's wrong is a bake change in `world-model/routing` (and a routing-golden re-pin). Its pure helpers (`wires/wire-looks`, `wires/wire-segments`, `wires/rope`) only turn the bake's integer tenths into instance data, coil rings and sleeve positions, and are lint-fenced.
- **One instanced mesh per look, never per wire.** A look is the insulation (or jacket) table key; colour is per instance. Adding a conductor with a new insulation adds one draw, not one per wire; give it a row in `WIRE_LOOK_FINISHES` or it gets the default finish. Unknown refs draw grey in the `unknown` look.
- **Wire pools rebuild, never resize.** `InstancedPool` (shared with the debug overlays) doubles when full and, for wires, shrinks to a quarter when a quarter is used; both build a new `InstancedMesh` with a new geometry and dispose the old pair. Anything that kept a reference to `pool.mesh` holds a disposed mesh after a rebuild: look it up again (`scene.getObjectByName('wires:<look>')`). The group's children are re-ordered after every change (looks by name, `wires:sleeves`, `wires:preview`), so the scene graph doesn't depend on history. Every write is a full `needsUpdate`: an ESLint rule bans `addUpdateRange` in the renderer, and the wire, pool and overlay specs spy `BufferAttribute.prototype.addUpdateRange`. With `shrink`, a pool at twice its minimum stays there even when empty (a quarter would be under the floor).
- **No wires, no wire resources.** The `wires` group is in the scene only while a route, sleeve or preview is drawn, and each mesh's geometry goes with it, so `wireResourceCount` (a mesh, its geometry and its material per look, the sleeves and the preview) reaching 0 is the disposal baseline and every scene-graph snapshot without wires is unchanged. The group leaves the scene before `disposeScene` runs (the controller registers it after the scene), or its geometries and materials would be disposed twice.
- **The wire renderer re-bakes on any change to `entities`, `connections` or `cables`** (by identity), then compares the bake with what it drew and writes nothing if it's the same. That makes a `device.operate` on a 500-wire plant cost a bake (about 2 ms) for no drawing change. If that shows in a profile, cache by the entities the bake reads (transforms, refs, raceway lengths); don't skip the bake on `entities` changes, or a moved terminal block leaves its wires behind.
- **Overlays tint conductors through `WireTint`, they don't draw them** (D15). `SimOverlays.setConductorTint(wires)` is wired by the controller; while voltage, current or heat is on the overlays hand the wire renderer a tint (each end's half in that end's step colour) and call `refreshTint()` when steps move, which rewrites instance colours only if a colour changed. Conductors are joined with their own `OverlaySteps` (the lead arrays stay leads only). An unlanded end reads node −1 (unknown). Unknown conductors differ from known ones by colour only (leads also draw thinner); the terminals at their ends keep the diamond markers. **A cable's jacket** has no reading, so while a tint is on it is drawn in the tint's `unknownColour`, never in its jacket colour, which could read as a value on the scale; since F3.2 part 4 its conductors' **tails** are drawn and each takes `colour(id, end)` for its own cable end along its whole length. **The half split is an abstraction:** each half takes its end's node voltage, while a real conductor's IR drop runs linearly along its length; the split at mid-length only shows which end is which.
- **The heat overlay shows temperatures whatever the record's `status`** (F3.6 part 3, `sim/heat-steps.ts`). Temperatures are own state, always written (ADR 0011, protocol 7): an `open` record (a wire with a free end, an intermittent end open this dwell, a fused conductor) still has real temperatures, so it is drawn by them, and a loose end that flickers open doesn't flicker to "no reading". No reading is only: no snapshot, no record for the connection in this descriptor (added since, or a kind the readout doesn't know, whose values read 0), or a conductor temperature that isn't a positive number. Cables draw unknown (no record for a jacket). Never map a missing temperature to bin 0: cold is a reading.
- **Each heat half is its hottest node, max(T_c, T_end)**, not the end's temperature alone. The conductor node is the whole body, so a half holds it and its end's hot spot; an end whose state is `none` (lead clips, a free end, a terminal with no method spec) has its temperature written 0 and is skipped. A hot spot really sits within about one fin length (about 84 mm on 14 AWG, ADR 0018 §3.3) of its terminal, so a long wire's whole half reads as hot as its end: that is the two-half D15 tint, and a third mid-span zone would be a `WireTint` change in the wire renderer.
- **Heat steps survive a new descriptor; voltage and current steps don't.** Heat is a per-connection channel (ADR 0011 amendment §6): on a new descriptor the overlays re-join by connection id (`joinHeat`) and keep the steps, so an intermittent toggle's recompile doesn't reset hysteresis. In tests, reuse one descriptor object across snapshots of one generation: a fresh `TopologyDescriptor` per snapshot is a new generation, which resets the voltage and current steps and requests a frame even when nothing moved.
- **While heat is on it owns the wire and lead colours.** Voltage and current keep their terminal markers and arrows, but conductor and lead halves show heat; switching heat off hands the colours back to whichever of voltage or current is on. Heat steps are computed on every snapshot (cheap, allocation-free) but count as a change only while heat is shown, so a heat change with heat off requests no frame.
- **Every named point on the heat scale is a knot at a whole bin, and its hysteresis is in kelvin** (`sim/heat-scale.ts`, realism review F1). The points are existing sourced constants: ambient, the catalogue's lowest insulation `ratedTempDryK`, the termination overheat point (ambient + 100 K), insulation damage (that rating + 40 K), the contact oxidation onset (149 °C), other metals' melting points (at the nearest whole bin) and the highest `meltingPointK` (over range); sim-core's thresholds reach the renderer as copies in `sim-protocol` (`connection-output.ts` for ambient and the overheat rise, `thermal-thresholds.ts` for the damage margin and the oxidation onset, the latter outside the protocol fingerprint because no record carries them), each pinned equal in sim-core's `connection-records.spec.ts`. Only the knot bins (12, 16, 18, 20) and the 0.5 K hysteresis are game-design. **This deviates from ADR 0011 §9.2's half-bin hysteresis for heat only:** above the overheat point a bin is tens of kelvin wide, so half-bin hysteresis held a loose end warming to 393.8 K at bin 15, below the 393.15 K overheat mark, until about 423 K (past insulation damage and the oxidation onset). With every named point a bin boundary and `HEAT_HYSTERESIS_K` = 0.5 K, crossing a point changes the step within 0.5 K either way; the spec ramps from ambient to past melting and back. Don't reintroduce `binWithHysteresis` for heat.
- **A heat scale problem never crashes the viewport.** `heatScale` omits a catalogue point that doesn't fit between its neighbours (an insulation rated at or above the overheat point, a metal melting below the oxidation onset) and the legend lists it as "Not marked: …"; it throws only when there is no scale at all (no rating or melting point, the fixed points not rising) or a row value has no source (never defaulted to real-world). `SimOverlays` catches that: heat is then unavailable, wires and leads show no reading, and the legend says why (`heatUnavailable`). The legend draws the overlays' own scale (`OverlayLegendState.heatLegend`), not the built-in one, so a modded catalogue's legend matches what is drawn.
- **One heat scale serves every wire, marked at the catalogue's lowest insulation rating.** Every P3 row is rated 90 °C, so this is exact today; once a catalogue mixes ratings (a 60 °C NM-B rule, 105 °C or 125 °C types), a wire with a higher rating reads "past the rating" too early, and the overlay needs a per-wire scale normalised to each wire's own rating (and its own insulation-damage point). Also: unknown grey (`#50555c`) and heat bin 0 (`#420a68`) are both dark, so cold and no reading are close in a dim scene; the legend names both in words, and a follow-up should separate them further.
- **The preview rope is the only simulated wire, and it is view state.** `setWirePreview({ pointsMm, ref, colour })` pins a `VerletRope`; `update()` steps it a fixed two substeps a frame (never `dt`-scaled), so a replay of frames gives the same shape, and returns `true` until it settles (about 20 frames), then the loop idles. A nearly taut Verlet rope stretches under gravity: the long-range tethers to each span's pins are what keep its sag near the bake's; don't remove them to save time.
- **Drawn diameters come from the lane maths, never from scaling in the renderer** (or wires and lanes disagree). Since F3.2 part 4 `laneSizeTenths` widens a stranded conductor's metal by the concentric bundle factor (`strandedDiameterFactor(strandCount)`: (2k + 1)/√n, 3/√7 for 7 strands); compact stranding isn't marked in the catalogue, so a compact conductor (the XHHW-2 aluminium entries) is drawn concentric, a few per cent wide (follow-up: a catalogue flag). No routing golden moved: the parity world and the bench are solid THHN. A stranded conductor in a future golden moves it.
- **Cables are drawn from two bakes, joined, never re-derived** (F3.2 part 4). The jacket is `bakeWorldRoutes`' run with `bakeCableEnds`' stubs on each end (`joinCableJacket` only concatenates); tails are `bakeCableEnds`' polylines. A wrong tail, fan or jacket end is a `routing/tails.ts` or `cableJacketEnd` change, not a renderer one. `bakeCableEnds` isn't in `centrelineHash` or the routing goldens (adding it would re-pin them); it has its own spec.
- **A tail is a drawn route whose owner is its conductor (`c<id>`), and a conductor can have two** (one per cable end). Picking, `drawn()` and the highlight key on the owner, so hovering either tail highlights both; `WireGraph` tells them apart by `tail`. Code that assumed one drawn route per owner (a `find` by owner) now needs the `tail` too.
- **Flat segments use a full basis, not a quaternion** (`flatSegmentMatrix`). The basis must stay right-handed (x × y = z) or the cylinder's faces flip winding and back-face culling hides them; the spec checks it per instance. The thin axis switches between +y and +z at |dy| = 0.9: a sagging span crossing that line would twist by 90° between two samples. P3's NM runs are horizontal or vertical, so it doesn't show; a smooth frame (parallel transport along the polyline) is the fix if it ever does.
- **Pickers (F3.2 part 3) follow ADR 0017 §9:** terminals are data points (`terminalPlace`), picked in screen space within `min(16 px, pitch / 2)` and filtered by the BVH occlusion test; waypoints go through `snapWaypoint` from a ray hit rounded to whole mm; wires through a BVH over baked segment bounds. Never raycast terminal meshes. Details: [rendering architecture](../architecture/rendering.md#pickers-and-the-routing-gesture-f32-part-3).
- **The routing gesture is the UI's; the renderer only draws it and reports picks.** `setRouting(session)` takes what the UI holds (spool, first terminal, waypoints); clicks, Enter and Escape come back as `RoutingEvent`s (`terminal`, `waypoint`, `cancel`), never as `ViewportIntent`s and never as commands. The UI logs exactly one `wire.add` per completed route, with the `proposal` the event carries. Don't give the renderer the session's state machine (Backspace, the second click completing a route): it would need `exec`.
- **No route maths in the renderer, including for the player's own waypoints.** The proposal is `proposeRoute` (`world-model` `routing/propose.ts`): no waypoints means exactly `autoRoute`; with waypoints it runs the command's own `checkRoute` and `checkGeometry`. The preview rope is drawn through the proposal's points, so what the player saw is what is logged. A new route rule goes into `world-model`, never into the controller.
- **The pick radius shrinks with the pitch, by design: there is no fixed radius to tune.** `pickScreenTerminal` picks the nearest visible terminal only within half its distance to the next visible one (capped at 16 px). So the dead zone between crowded terminals is deliberate (ADR 0010: the spike's fixed 12 px picked the neighbour), and `state.terminal.near` with `hit: null` is the cue for the zoom offer. Don't "fix" a miss there by widening the radius.
- **Terminal occlusion trusts the terminal's own device, within 15 mm.** The BVH picker's first hit on the ray through the terminal must be nothing, its own device no more than 15 mm in front (`TERMINAL_OWN_DEVICE_TOLERANCE_M`), or anything else within 3 mm in front (`TERMINAL_OCCLUSION_TOLERANCE_M`): entry points sit on or inside a body face (a feed-through block's clamp entry is in its top face, 11 mm behind the front), so a few mm for the own device fails head-on (measured: up to 11.3 mm on the routing panel at 1.2 m). Unbounded, a terminal on the far side of its own body (about 44 mm through a block) was pickable through it. Seen steeply from the side, a neighbouring block's body is a real occluder and the terminal stops being pickable: zoom (`view.focus` frames the device head-on) or Tab to it, which ignores occlusion.
- **Occlusion is asked lazily and memoised per hover pass** (`visibleMemo`): nearest candidate first, then only `near`'s neighbours within 48 px. Each ask is one `Picker.pick` (it allocates a little, as before); never test every terminal, and never call it outside a pass whose projection is current (`projectTerminals()` resets the memo).
- **A device is a work-mode focus target since F3.2 part 3** (`SelectableInfo.focusNormal` on catalogue devices), so `F` and `view.focus entity-<id>` frame it. The info's normal is only a "focusable" flag (it is set when the entity is first drawn); `applyFocus` re-reads the frame from the entity's current orientation (`deviceFocusFrame`), so a turned device is framed right. The initial focus stays a fixture (`focusInitial` skips entities).
- **Gotcha: a camera on its own zoom clamp never settles.** For a small target the framing distance used to be `minDistance` itself; a rounding error then made OrbitControls re-clamp the radius on every `update()`, which reported a change every frame, so the on-demand loop never went idle and e2e `whenIdle()` hung (found focusing a 6 mm terminal block). `workOrbitFor` now keeps `minDistance` below the framing distance (half of it for targets closer than 0.15 m). Any new camera limit must not equal the pose it starts in.
- **Unit tests that aim the camera by hand must aim OrbitControls too.** `update()` runs `OrbitControls.update()`, which re-aims the camera at its own `target` (the origin before any focus). `routing.spec.ts` sets the private `orbit.target` and calls `orbit.update()`; a world with fixtures can use `focus()` instead.
- **Wires are hovered and selected outside the `SelectionModel`.** `state.hoveredWire` / `state.selectedWire` (a `WireRef`) are exclusive with the entity selection (selecting either clears the other; Escape clears a wire first), so selection cycling, `selection.*` context keys and every e2e that counts selectables are unchanged. A wire beats the device the BVH picker hits unless that device is more than 2 mm nearer. Wires aren't picked while routing or placing.
- **The wire BVH is the pure `SegmentBvh`, not three-mesh-bvh,** so it is lint-fenced and unit-tested against a brute-force reference (`segment-bvh.spec.ts`). It is rebuilt lazily on the first pick after the drawn wires change; the segment arrays are written in `writeRoutes` alongside the instance matrices, so the picked shape is always the drawn one. The widened radius depends on depth (`pxAngle × depth`), so the node test grows each box by the radius at the box's far side.
- **Routing markers and the wire highlight follow the "no resources when unused" rule.** The `routing` group (one instanced sphere mesh, drawn over everything) is in the scene only while a marker shows, and `wires:highlight` exists only while a drawn wire is hovered or selected; `routingResourceCount` and `wireResourceCount` reach 0, and every scene-graph snapshot without a gesture or highlight is unchanged. The highlight sits after the sleeves and before the preview in the `wires` group's fixed child order.
